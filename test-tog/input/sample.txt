Marie Curie was a Polish-French physicist and chemist who conducted pioneering research on radioactivity. She was the first woman to win a Nobel Prize, and the only person to win the Nobel Prize in two different sciences (Physics in 1903 and Chemistry in 1911).

Marie Curie was born in Warsaw, Poland in 1867. She studied at the University of Paris, where she met her husband Pierre Curie. Together, they discovered the radioactive elements polonium and radium. Polonium was named after Poland, Marie's home country.

Pierre Curie was a French physicist who worked alongside his wife Marie Curie. He shared the 1903 Nobel Prize in Physics with Marie Curie and Henri Becquerel for their work on radioactivity. Pierre Curie died in 1906 in a street accident in Paris.

Henri Becquerel was a French physicist who discovered radioactivity in 1896 when he found that uranium emitted radiation. He shared the 1903 Nobel Prize in Physics with Marie and Pierre Curie.

The University of Paris, also known as La Sorbonne, is a prestigious French university where Marie Curie became the first female professor in 1906. The university is located in Paris, France.

Radioactivity is the spontaneous emission of radiation from unstable atomic nuclei. The study of radioactivity was pioneered by Marie Curie, Pierre Curie, and Henri Becquerel. Their discoveries led to major advances in nuclear physics and medicine.

The Nobel Prize in Physics 1903 was awarded jointly to Henri Becquerel and Pierre Curie and Marie Curie for their research on radioactivity. This was the first Nobel Prize awarded to a woman.

The Nobel Prize in Chemistry 1911 was awarded to Marie Curie for her discovery of radium and polonium. This made her the only person to win Nobel Prizes in two different scientific fields.
